# Campaign plan

A campaign is a run of pieces that push one outcome with one message. It is run here by one person, by hand, next to building the product, so the plan has to be small enough to actually happen.

## Decide these before writing anything

1. **Goal.** One outcome, stated so it can be checked afterwards: a number of requests, sign-ups, replies, downloads. Take it from the brief; fall back to the brand's goals. If the brief names no number, state the outcome and say that the founder should set the target.
2. **Audience.** Who exactly, from the brand's audience. A campaign for everyone reaches no one.
3. **Message.** The single thing the reader should believe at the end, in one or two sentences. Every piece carries it from a different side. If two pieces could swap places without anyone noticing, one of them is not needed.
4. **Ask.** The one action the campaign leads to, and where it happens. Check that the place exists: read the page. If it does not, that is a site task, and it comes before the first post.

## Shape of the weeks

Lay the window out in phases, each with a job:

- **Open** with the problem or the observation, not the product. Give the reader a reason to keep watching.
- **Show** the substance: one concrete thing at a time, with evidence.
- **Ask** plainly once the substance is on the table, then again near the end for those who saw only part of it.

Default pace is two or three posts a week on the main channel, on weekdays, in the audience's working hours. More than that is not sustainable for one person and reads as noise. Leave gaps; a missed day should not break the sequence.

## When the campaign rolls out materials

Materials such as catalogs each cover a part of the product. Treat each one as its own moment, not as an attachment to a generic post.

1. **Read them all first.** Note for each: what it covers, the strongest single fact or claim in it, who in the audience it matters to most, and what it assumes the reader already knows.
2. **Order them by what the reader needs first.** Start with the one that answers the audience's most common problem or that the others build on. Leave the most specialised for later. Do not order them by file name.
3. **Give each a launch post and one follow-up.** The launch post says what the material is and why this reader should open it, leading with its strongest fact. The follow-up, a few days later, takes one specific finding or example from inside it and stands as a post without the material. That is two posts per material; more only if the material is unusually rich.
4. **Space them.** One material per week is a comfortable pace; two if the window is short. Never two launches on the same day.
5. **Connect them.** From the second material on, a post may point back to an earlier one in half a sentence, so that someone who joins late can find the start. Close the campaign with one post that lists all of them and repeats the ask.
6. **Name the attachment** on every post that announces or draws on a material, so the founder knows which file to share with it.
7. **Stay inside the material.** Quote its numbers with their source and scope as the material gives them. If a material makes a claim the brand rules would not allow (an unsupported superlative, a number without a source), leave the claim out of the post and mention it in your closing note.

## Each post

Follow the social-post skill. In the rationale say which phase the post belongs to and what job it does there, so the founder can judge the plan and not only the wording.

## Measuring

This system sees no analytics. Give every link in the campaign a tag the founder can filter on later, in the form the site already uses (for example `?ref=linkedin`); read a page to see what form that is. Say in the closing note what to look at after the campaign, and where.

## What to leave out

- A plan of titles without the posts written. Every slot is a finished draft.
- Channels the brand does not use. Follow the brand notes on what may appear where.
- Features that are not released. Announce only what the materials, the site or the brand description say exists.
